🔬 Pathophysiology — Dissociated Sensory Loss
  • Syringomyelia: Cavity in central cord destroys crossing spinothalamic fibers (at that level) → pain/temp loss in "cape" distribution. Dorsal columns intact → JPS preserved
  • Brown-Séquard (cord hemisection): Ipsilateral: corticospinal (motor ↓) + dorsal column (JPS ↓). Contralateral: spinothalamic (pain/temp ↓) — because spinothalamic decussates 1–2 levels above entry
  • Subacute Combined Degeneration (B12↓): Dorsal columns + lateral corticospinal tracts → proprioception ↓ + UMN signs + cognitive change
⭐ Sensory Exam Points
  • Romberg positive = sensory ataxia (not cerebellar — cerebellar Romberg usually negative)
  • Vibration lost at great toe → test at knee, then hip to find level (large fiber neuropathy)

🔬 Stroke Pathophysiology
  • Ischemic cascade: Occlusion → ATP depletion (30s) → Na/K pump failure → cell swelling → glutamate release → NMDA-mediated Ca²⁺ influx → mitochondrial failure → apoptosis/necrosis
  • Penumbra: Salvageable tissue around infarct core (CBF 10–20 mL/100g/min). Survives ~4–6h. Target of reperfusion therapy
  • Hemorrhagic transformation: Blood-brain barrier breakdown after ischemia → reperfusion injury. Risk ↑ with large infarcts, delayed thrombolysis, anticoagulation
  • Lacunar mechanism: Lipohyalinosis of small penetrating arteries (chronic HTN) → microatheroma or embolism
🖼️ CT/MRI Findings
  • CT acute (<6h): Hyperdense MCA sign (clot), loss of grey-white differentiation, sulcal effacement, ASPECTS score
  • DWI bright + ADC dark = cytotoxic edema = acute infarct (within minutes of onset)
  • FLAIR: Hyperintense = subacute/chronic (>4–6h). DWI-FLAIR mismatch → recent stroke (<4.5h)
  • GRE/SWI: "Blooming" hypointensity = hemosiderin, hemorrhage, microbleeds
  • MRA: Vessel cut-off or filling defect at occlusion site
  • CVST on MRI: Loss of flow void in sinus; T1 bright clot (subacute). MRV: filling defect/absent flow
⭐ Stroke Imaging Exam Points
  • Wallenberg: DWI restriction lateral medulla. No pyramidal tract involvement → NO hemiplegia
  • ASPECTS score: 10 = normal CT; <6 = large infarct, poor outcome with thrombolysis
  • DWI-FLAIR mismatch (DWI bright, FLAIR normal) = wake-up stroke eligible for treatment within 4.5h window
  • CN VI false localizing sign: Long intracranial course → stretched by ↑ICP → not necessarily pontine lesion

🔬 Pathophysiology
  • Toxoplasma: T. gondii reactivation (latent cysts) → focal necrotizing encephalitis with central caseous necrosis → ring of inflammatory cells + peripheral edema
  • PML: JC virus (polyomavirus) infects oligodendrocytes → demyelination without inflammation → no BBB breakdown → no enhancement, no mass effect
  • Cryptococcus: Polysaccharide capsule evades phagocytosis → "gelatinous pseudocysts" in Virchow-Robin spaces of basal ganglia → obstructive hydrocephalus
  • TB meningitis: Basal exudate → endarteritis → lacunar infarcts + hydrocephalus + CN palsies
🖼️ Imaging Findings
  • Toxoplasma MRI: Multiple ring-enhancing lesions at grey-white junction and basal ganglia. "Target sign" (concentric rings on T1+Gd). Mass effect + edema
  • PML MRI: Subcortical and periventricular white matter T2/FLAIR hyperintensity. No gadolinium enhancement. No mass effect. Scalloped grey-white interface
  • Cryptococcal MRI: Gelatinous pseudocysts (T2 bright) in basal ganglia/thalamus. Leptomeningeal enhancement. Communicating hydrocephalus
  • TB meningitis CT/MRI: Basal meningeal enhancement, hydrocephalus, periventricular lucencies (transependymal CSF), lacunar infarcts (basal ganglia)

⭐ Autoimmune Points
  • NMOSD: Avoid beta-interferon/natalizumab (worsen). Maintenance: rituximab/azathioprine
  • McDonald 2017 (MS): DIS + DIT. OCB in CSF = DIT even without 2nd attack
  • LGI1: Faciobrachial dystonic seizures (FBDS) before encephalitis + hyponatremia
🔬 NMOSD Pathophysiology
  • AQP4-IgG (NMO-IgG): Targets aquaporin-4 water channels on astrocyte end-feet at blood-brain barrier → complement activation → astrocyte destruction → demyelination secondary to astrocytopathy
  • MOG-IgG: Targets myelin oligodendrocyte glycoprotein → outer surface of myelin → oligodendrocyte damage → distinct from AQP4 disease (MOGAD)
  • MS pathophysiology: Autoreactive T cells cross BBB → perivenular inflammation → demyelination (acute) → axonal loss (chronic progressive). Oligoclonal bands = intrathecal IgG synthesis
🖼️ Imaging Findings
  • NMOSD spine MRI: T2 hyperintensity ≥3 vertebral segments, central cord, often involves entire cord cross-section. Cord swelling + gadolinium enhancement (acute)
  • NMOSD brain MRI: Often normal early. Area postrema lesion (dorsal medulla) — pathognomonic. Hypothalamus, periaqueductal involvement
  • MS MRI: "Dawson fingers" — periventricular lesions oriented perpendicular to ventricles (on sagittal FLAIR). Juxtacortical, infratentorial, spinal cord plaques. Gadolinium-enhancing = active
  • Anti-NMDA encephalitis: Often normal MRI. Subtle FLAIR cortical/subcortical signal in some. FLAIR DWI changes in hippocampus

🔬 MG Pathophysiology
  • AChR-Ab (85%): IgG1/IgG3 bind nicotinic AChR at neuromuscular junction → complement-mediated destruction + accelerated internalization → reduced functional AChR density → impaired end-plate potentials → fatigable weakness
  • MuSK-Ab (10%): Disrupts MuSK (muscle-specific kinase) → AChR clustering failure → NMJ structural disorganization. Bulbar-predominant, seronegative for AChR-Ab
  • LEMS: VGCC-Ab blocks presynaptic P/Q-type voltage-gated calcium channels → ↓ ACh vesicle release → weakness. Post-tetanic potentiation → reflexes improve after exercise
🖼️ MG/GBS Imaging
  • MG — CT chest: Anterior mediastinal mass = thymoma (10–15%). Thymic hyperplasia more common (~65%). CT chest mandatory in ALL newly diagnosed MG
  • GBS — MRI spine: Gadolinium enhancement of anterior spinal nerve roots ("cauda equina enhancement"). Not always present; helps confirm when CSF normal early
  • GBS — NCS: Demyelinating pattern: prolonged distal latencies, slowed conduction velocity, conduction block, prolonged F-waves (AIDP). AMAN: axonal pattern (reduced CMAP amplitude)
  • POEMS — PET/bone survey: Sclerotic bone lesions (osteosclerotic myeloma). VEGF markedly elevated (>200 pg/mL)
⭐ Neuromuscular High-Yield Points
  • GBS respiratory: Intubate if FVC <15–20 mL/kg, NIF <–25 cmH2O, or 30% drop from baseline ("20-30-40 rule")
  • MG crisis triggers: Aminoglycosides, quinolones, beta-blockers, iodinated contrast, hyper-Mg. Avoid all in MG
  • MFS (Miller Fisher): Anti-GQ1b IgG; ophthalmoplegia + ataxia + areflexia. Overlap with Bickerstaff encephalitis (add encephalopathy)

Bradykinesia (mandatory)Core — required per MDS 2015

MDS 2015 PD Criteria
Sensitivity 94.5%, Specificity 88.5% · Bradykinesia MANDATORY + ≥1 of (rest tremor or rigidity) → apply exclusion/red flags/supportive criteria

Red Flags Against Idiopathic PD

  • Early falls (<3 years)
  • Poor/no levodopa response
  • Symmetric onset
  • Early dementia (<1 year of motor onset)
  • Early severe autonomic failure
  • Downgaze palsy → PSP
  • Cerebellar signs → MSA-C
🔬 PD Pathophysiology
  • Alpha-synuclein misfolding: SNCA mutations or environmental triggers → α-syn aggregation → Lewy bodies and Lewy neurites in dopaminergic neurons of substantia nigra pars compacta (SNpc)
  • Braak staging (1–6): Stage 1–2: Brainstem (dorsal vagal nucleus, olfactory) → prodromal (constipation, anosmia, RBD). Stage 3–4: SNpc, amygdala → motor symptoms appear. Stage 5–6: Neocortex → dementia (PDD)
  • 50–60% SNpc neuron loss at motor symptom onset — long preclinical phase
  • Direct pathway (D1 receptor): Striatum → GPi (inhibit) → Thalamus disinhibited → movement facilitated. In PD: ↓ dopamine → direct pathway underactive → movement inhibited
  • Indirect pathway (D2 receptor): Striatum → GPe → STN → GPi. In PD: ↑ STN activity → ↑ GPi inhibition → thalamus suppressed → bradykinesia/rigidity
🖼️ PD Imaging
  • MRI brain: Usually normal in idiopathic PD. May show SN hyperechogenicity on TCS (transcranial sonography)
  • DAT-SPECT (DaTscan): ↓ striatal dopamine transporter uptake — "comma" → "period" pattern. Distinguishes degenerative parkinsonism from essential tremor/drug-induced
  • PSP — MRI: Hummingbird sign (midbrain atrophy on sagittal T1) — midbrain AP diameter <12mm. "Mickey Mouse" on axial (midbrain < pons). T2 signal change in subthalamic nucleus
  • MSA — MRI: Hot cross bun sign (T2 pons — cruciform pontine hyperintensity). Putaminal slit sign (lateral T2 hypointensity with dorsolateral rim). Cerebellar atrophy (MSA-C)
  • DLB — DaT-SPECT: ↓ putamen uptake (differentiates from AD where DaT normal). FDG-PET: hypometabolism occipital cortex (spared in AD)
⭐ Atypical Parkinsonism Imaging Points
  • PSP hummingbird sign: Midbrain area <70 mm² on axial MRI. Midbrain:pons area ratio <0.12
  • MSA hot cross bun: Cruciform T2 hyperintensity in pons from degeneration of transverse pontocerebellar fibers and pontine raphe
  • DLB: Avoid typical antipsychotics (neuroleptic sensitivity — can precipitate NMS-like crisis)
  • CBD asymmetric cortical atrophy: Contralateral to clinically affected limb. "Alien limb" phenomenon + cortical sensory loss + apraxia

🔬 Cerebellar Circuit Pathophysiology
  • Cerebellar cortex: Purkinje cells (GABA-ergic) → deep cerebellar nuclei (DCN) → thalamus → motor cortex. Purkinje cells tonically inhibit DCN
  • Climbing fibers (inferior olive → Purkinje): Error correction signal. Damaged in paraneoplastic (anti-Yo), alcohol, MSA-C
  • SCA pathophysiology: CAG trinucleotide repeat expansions in ATXN genes → polyglutamine tracts → protein misfolding → Purkinje cell degeneration. Longer repeat = earlier onset (anticipation)
  • Friedreich's ataxia: GAA intronic repeat (FXN gene, AR) → frataxin deficiency → mitochondrial iron accumulation → oxidative stress → dorsal root ganglia + spinocerebellar tract degeneration + cardiomyopathy
  • Wilson's disease: ATP7B mutation → impaired biliary copper excretion → hepatic copper accumulation → overflow into blood → deposition in brain (basal ganglia, thalamus, brainstem), liver, cornea (KF rings), kidneys
🖼️ Ataxia Imaging
  • SCA MRI: Cerebellar atrophy (vermis and hemispheres), +/– brainstem atrophy. Some SCAs have pontine atrophy (SCA2, SCA3)
  • MSA-C MRI: Hot cross bun sign (pons T2), middle cerebellar peduncle (MCP) T2 hyperintensity, cerebellar atrophy, pontine atrophy
  • Friedreich's MRI: Cervical cord atrophy (dorsal columns/lateral corticospinal tracts). Minimal cerebellar atrophy relatively. Heart MRI: concentric hypertrophic cardiomyopathy
  • Wilson's MRI: "Giant panda" face (T2 axial midbrain — hyperintense tectal plate + bilateral red nuclei + preserved SN = panda face). T2 hyperintensity putamen, thalamus, brainstem, dentate nuclei. T1 hypointensity (copper deposition)
  • Wernicke's MRI: Symmetric T2/FLAIR hyperintensity at periaqueductal grey, mammillary bodies, medial thalami. DWI restriction in acute phase

⭐ PD Drug Exam Points
  • Levodopa protein interaction: Large neutral amino acids (LNAA) compete at jejunal transport → take LD 30–45 min before meals
  • 90% motor fluctuations at 10 yr · 60% dyskinesias at 10 yr
  • DBS targets: STN (reduces motor complications) or GPi (better for dyskinesia). Requires good levodopa response pre-op
  • Selegiline + pethidine = absolute contraindication (serotonin syndrome)
🧠 Basal Ganglia Circuit — Interactive Pathophysiology
🟢 Normal Circuit
Dopamine from SNpc modulates both pathways.

Direct (D1): Striatum→GPi↓ → Thalamus disinhibited → movement facilitated.

Indirect (D2): Striatum→GPe↓ → STN released → GPi excited → Thalamus suppressed.

DA facilitates D1 (more movement) and inhibits D2 (less suppression) → net: movement enabled.
⭐ Basal Ganglia Exam Points
  • PD: ↓DA → ↓D1 direct (GPi not suppressed) + ↓D2 inhibition (STN overactive → GPi overactive) → net ↑GPi → Thalamus suppressed → bradykinesia + rigidity
  • STN-DBS: High-frequency stimulation suppresses overactive STN → ↓GPi output → Thalamus released → movement restored
  • GPi-DBS: Direct GPi suppression — preferred when dyskinesia is dominant (less wearing-off fluctuation)
  • Levodopa protein competition: LNAA compete at jejunal transport → take 30 min before meals · 90% of levodopa is peripherally decarboxylated → combine with carbidopa/benserazide
  • Hemiballismus: STN lesion (stroke) → GPi under-driven → Thalamus disinhibited → wild flinging movements contralateral side

🔬 PSP Pathophysiology
  • 4-repeat tau (4R-tau) accumulation → globose NFTs in neurons + astrocytic tufts in glia
  • Affects: subthalamic nucleus, GP, midbrain, frontal cortex, pontine nuclei
  • Richardson syndrome: vertical gaze palsy (downgaze) + early falls + frontal dementia
  • PSP-P variant: mimics PD early but poor levodopa response, falls within 3 yr
🖼️ PSP MRI Findings
  • Hummingbird sign (sagittal T1): selective midbrain atrophy — thin beak on large pons ← PATHOGNOMONIC
  • Mickey Mouse sign (axial T1): small round midbrain gives concave lateral borders
  • Morning glory sign: T2 signal in STN bilaterally
  • Midbrain:Pons area ratio <0.12 (Sens 100%, Spec 96%)
  • FDG-PET: frontal + midbrain hypometabolism
⭐ PSP Exam Points
  • Vertical gaze palsy (downgaze first) — hallmark bedside sign; ask patient "look at my chin"
  • Early postural instability → falls backwards (<1 year onset)
  • Midbrain AP <12mm / area <70mm² / ratio <0.12 on MRI
  • Tau = 4R-tau (vs 3R in Pick's, mixed 3R+4R in AD)
  • Poor levodopa response; no DBS benefit

🔬 MSA Pathophysiology
  • Alpha-synuclein in oligodendrocytes → glial cytoplasmic inclusions (GCIs) ← hallmark
  • MSA-C: olivopontocerebellar atrophy → cerebellar ataxia dominant
  • MSA-P: striatonigral degeneration → parkinsonism dominant
  • Both: severe early autonomic failure (bladder, orthostatic hypotension, sweating)
  • Cerebellar + Parkinsonism + Autonomic in one patient = think MSA
🖼️ MSA MRI Findings
  • Hot cross bun sign (pons T2): cruciform hyperintensity — degeneration of transverse pontocerebellar fibers + pontine raphe ← PATHOGNOMONIC for MSA-C
  • Putaminal slit sign (T2/GRE): lateral hypointensity (iron) + dorsolateral bright rim (reactive gliosis) ← PATHOGNOMONIC for MSA-P
  • MCP hyperintensity: middle cerebellar peduncle T2 bright
  • Cerebellar + pontine atrophy, vermis > hemispheres
⭐ MSA Exam Points
  • Cerebellar signs + Parkinsonism + early severe autonomic = MSA until proven otherwise
  • Hot cross bun = pons T2 cruciform (transverse pontocerebellar fiber degeneration)
  • Putaminal slit = lateral T2↓ with bright rim on T2 or GRE
  • Poor levodopa response (unlike PD); No DBS benefit
  • Synucleinopathy (same family as PD + DLB)

🔬 Wilson's Pathophysiology
  • ATP7B mutation (AR, Chr13q) → impaired biliary copper excretion
  • Copper accumulates: liver → overflow → blood (↓ceruloplasmin) → deposits in brain, cornea (KF rings), kidneys, RBCs
  • Brain copper: basal ganglia (putamen>caudate), thalamus, brainstem, dentate
  • Movement: dystonia, wing-beating tremor, dysarthria, dysphagia, parkinsonism
  • Psychiatric: personality change, psychosis, depression (50% present psychiatrically)
🖼️ Wilson's MRI Findings
  • Giant panda face (T2 axial midbrain): RN hyperintense (eyes) + SN preserved (nose) + tegmentum bright (forehead) ← PATHOGNOMONIC
  • Double panda: pons also involved → midbrain panda + pontine panda
  • T2↑: putamen, caudate, thalamus, brainstem, dentate nuclei
  • T1↓: putamen (paramagnetic copper deposition)
  • CT: hyperdensity basal ganglia (copper); liver: cirrhosis pattern
⭐ Wilson's Exam Points
  • Young patient (<40) liver disease + neuropsychiatric symptoms → always check Wilson's
  • KF rings on slit lamp (97% neurological Wilson's) — check this in the exam!
  • Diagnosis: ceruloplasmin↓ + 24h urine Cu↑ + liver biopsy Cu↑ (gold std)
  • Giant panda face = T2 axial midbrain (red nuclei = eyes)
  • Rx: D-penicillamine (chelation) or trientine; or zinc (blocks absorption); liver transplant = curative
  • AR inheritance — screen all siblings

🔬 DaT-SPECT Mechanism
  • ¹²³I-ioflupane (DaTscan): SPECT radiotracer binding to dopamine transporter (DAT) on presynaptic nigrostriatal terminals
  • DAT density = surrogate marker for nigrostriatal neuron integrity
  • PD: ↓ DAT in putamen (more) then caudate (later) — caudal → rostral loss
  • ET: Normal DaT (tremor is non-degenerative) ← key diagnostic tool
  • Drug-induced parkinsonism: Normal DaT (postsynaptic receptor blockade)
🖼️ DaT-SPECT Patterns
  • Normal: symmetric "comma" or "D" bilateral — full caudate + putamen
  • Early PD: asymmetric putamen ↓ (contralateral to symptomatic side) → comma with short tail
  • Advanced PD: bilateral putamen ↓↓ → only caudate dots = "period sign"
  • DLB: bilateral ↓ (differentiates from AD where DaT is normal)
  • MSA/PSP: DaT ↓ but does NOT distinguish from PD
⭐ DaT-SPECT Exam Points
  • Differentiates degenerative parkinsonism (PD, MSA, PSP, DLB) from ET and drug-induced parkinsonism
  • Does NOT distinguish PD from MSA/PSP/DLB (all show ↓ DaT)
  • Normal DaT + parkinsonian tremor = ET → avoid dopaminergic drugs
  • DLB: ↓ DaT = core imaging biomarker (equivalent to amyloid-PET for AD)
  • Not needed if PD diagnosis clear clinically (MDS criteria met)

🔬 DLB Pathophysiology
  • Alpha-synuclein Lewy bodies in brainstem AND neocortex (unlike PD limited to brainstem early)
  • Visual hallucinations: Lewy bodies in occipital/visual association cortex → FDG-PET hypometabolism
  • REM sleep behavior disorder: brainstem Lewy body accumulation (locus coeruleus)
  • Fluctuating cognition: thalamic + cholinergic nucleus basalis of Meynert dysfunction
  • Parkinsonism: nigrostriatal degeneration (same as PD)
🖼️ DLB Imaging Findings
  • FDG-PET: occipital hypometabolism (visual cortex) ← HALLMARK, differentiates DLB from AD where occipital is spared early
  • DaT-SPECT: ↓ striatal DAT ← core biomarker (differentiates DLB from AD)
  • MIBG cardiac scintigraphy: ↓ cardiac sympathetic innervation (H/M ratio <2.2) — sensitive + specific for DLB/PD synucleinopathy
  • MRI: often normal or mild generalized atrophy (hippocampus less affected than AD)
⭐ DLB Exam Points
  • 4 Core features: fluctuating cognition / recurrent vivid visual hallucinations / RBD / parkinsonism → 2+ core = probable DLB
  • CRITICAL: AVOID typical antipsychotics → neuroleptic hypersensitivity (can precipitate life-threatening parkinsonism/NMS-like)
  • Treat psychosis: clozapine or quetiapine only
  • DaT-SPECT ↓ = core imaging biomarker; MIBG ↓ = supportive
  • FDG-PET occipital↓ differentiates from AD (occipital spared in AD early)
  • ChEI (rivastigmine) for cognitive symptoms — FDA approved

Normal State
  • CBF: ~50 mL/100g/min (grey matter), ~20 mL/100g/min (white matter)
  • Cerebral autoregulation: Maintains CBF over MAP 50–150 mmHg. Fails in severe stroke → pressure-passive flow
  • Energy consumption: Brain uses 20% of body O₂ despite being 2% of body weight
⭐ Stroke Cascade Key Points
  • Core: CBF <10 mL/100g/min → irreversible infarction within minutes. DWI bright + ADC dark
  • Penumbra: CBF 10–20 mL/100g/min → electrically silent but viable for 4–6h. Perfusion-diffusion mismatch on MRI = target for reperfusion
  • Glutamate excitotoxicity: Na⁺/K⁺ pump fails → depolarization → glutamate release → NMDA receptor activation → Ca²⁺ influx → mitochondrial damage → apoptosis cascade
  • Penumbra is time-dependent: "Time is brain" — ~1.9 million neurons lost per minute in untreated MCA stroke
  • Hemorrhagic transformation risk: ↑ with large infarct area, late reperfusion (>6h), anticoagulation, malignant MCA syndrome
🔌 Neuromuscular Junction — Normal vs MG vs LEMS
State:
Normal NMJ
  • Action potential arrives at motor neuron terminal → opens voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels (VGCC)
  • Ca²⁺ influx triggers exocytosis of ACh vesicles into synaptic cleft
  • ACh binds nicotinic AChR (α-subunit) on postsynaptic membrane → Na⁺ influx → end-plate potential → muscle contraction
  • AChE (acetylcholinesterase) rapidly degrades ACh in cleft → terminates signal
📊 MG vs LEMS — RNS Patterns

RNS at 3 Hz (Low-rate)

Why the Different RNS Patterns?
  • MG — decremental at 3Hz: Each stimulus depletes ACh vesicles from readily releasable pool. With fewer functional AChR (blocked by Ab), the safety factor falls below threshold → decremental CMAP. Exercise briefly improves (post-activation facilitation) then worsens (post-activation exhaustion)
  • LEMS — incremental at 50Hz: VGCC-Ab reduces Ca²⁺ entry → minimal ACh released at rest → low amplitude CMAP. High-frequency stimulation → Ca²⁺ accumulates intracellularly → facilitates more ACh release → CMAP amplitude increases ≥100%
  • SFEMG: Most sensitive test for MG. Increased "jitter" (variability in interpotential interval) = NMJ transmission instability
🧲 Demyelination — Saltatory Conduction vs Demyelinated Nerve
State:
Normal Saltatory Conduction
  • Myelin sheath (produced by oligodendrocytes in CNS, Schwann cells in PNS) insulates axon between nodes
  • Nodes of Ranvier have high density of voltage-gated Na⁺ channels → action potential regenerates only at nodes → "jumps" from node to node
  • Advantages: ~70 m/s conduction velocity (vs ~1 m/s unmyelinated), energy efficient (fewer Na⁺/K⁺ pump cycles needed)
🔬 MS Demyelination Pathophysiology
  • Perivenular inflammation: CD4+ T cells (Th1/Th17) cross BBB → activate macrophages and microglia → attack myelin via molecular mimicry (MBP, PLP, MOG)
  • Acute plaque: BBB breakdown → gadolinium enhancement → lymphocytes + macrophages stripping myelin → relative axon preservation
  • Demyelinated axon: Cannot support saltatory conduction → continuous conduction attempted along entire axon → fails (Na⁺ channel redistribution needed) → conduction block
  • Uhthoff phenomenon: Heat ↓ already-marginal conduction in demyelinated axons → symptoms worsen with exercise/hot baths. Pathognomonic for demyelination
  • Remyelination: Oligodendrocyte precursors can remyelinate → thin myelin ("shadow plaques") → partial recovery. Fails in chronic/progressive MS
  • NCS in demyelinating neuropathy (GBS/CIDP): ↑ distal motor latency, ↓ NCV, conduction block, prolonged F-waves. Amplitude relatively preserved early
🖼️ Demyelination Imaging
  • MS "Dawson fingers": Periventricular FLAIR/T2 lesions oriented perpendicular to ventricles (along medullary veins) — best seen on sagittal FLAIR
  • MS lesion locations (McDonald 2017 DIS): Periventricular (≥3) + juxtacortical/cortical + infratentorial + spinal cord (any number)
  • Active MS plaque: T1 gadolinium-enhancing (open ring or solid). T2 hyperintense. T1 hypointense "black holes" = chronic axon loss
  • NMOSD cord: "Pencil-thin" T2 signal, central, ≥3 vertebral segments. Often entire cord cross-section involved (vs partial in MS)
  • GBS MRI: Anterior cauda equina nerve root enhancement (gadolinium). Dorsal root enhancement less common. Not always present in early GBS
  • CIDP MRI nerves: Enlarged, T2-hyperintense peripheral nerves on MRI neurography. "Onion bulb" on nerve biopsy (repeated demyelination-remyelination)

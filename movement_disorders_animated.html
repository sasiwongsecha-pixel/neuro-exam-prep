⚡ Circuit-Level Pathophysiology
How basal ganglia and cerebellar circuits break down in movement disorders
🧠 Basal Ganglia Motor Circuit — Direct vs Indirect Pathway
Basal Ganglia Circuit Simulator
D1 direct → promotes movement | D2 indirect → suppresses movement | Dopamine balances both

Normal Balance

Direct (D1-MSN): Striatum → GPi↓ → Thalamus released → Cortex activated ✅ movement
Indirect (D2-MSN): Striatum → GPe↓ → STN↑ → GPi↑ → Thalamus suppressed ❌ movement
Dopamine: Excites D1 (direct ↑) AND Inhibits D2 (indirect ↓) → net facilitates movement

Parkinson's Disease — DA Loss

50–60% SNc neurons lost at motor onset!
• D1 underactive → Direct pathway ↓ (less thalamic release)
• D2 overactive → Indirect pathway ↑ (more thalamic suppression)
→ GPi hyperactive → Thalamus suppressed → Bradykinesia + Rigidity
DBS of STN: High-frequency stimulation "silences" STN → reduces GPi overactivity → restores thalamo-cortical drive → motor improvement

Lewy Body Progression (Braak Stages)

⭐ High-Yield Exam Points — Basal Ganglia
  • How many % of SNc neurons lost at motor onset? → 50–60%
  • D1 receptor → direct pathway (excites, promotes movement)
  • D2 receptor → indirect pathway (inhibits when activated, suppresses indirect)
  • GPi/SNr = output nucleus; tonically inhibits thalamus (GABA)
  • DBS target for PD motor fluctuations = STN or GPi
  • Braak staging: Prodromal (1–2) → Motor (3–4) → Cognitive (5–6)
🎯 Cerebellar Circuit — How Ataxia Develops
Cerebellar Cortex → DCN → Thalamo-Cortical Loop
Purkinje cells integrate parallel + climbing fiber input → rhythmically inhibit DCN → Thalamus pulsed → Smooth movement

The Olivocerebellar Module

The key reciprocal circuit: Cerebellar cortex ↔ Deep Cerebellar Nuclei (DCN) ↔ Inferior Olive

Purkinje cells = sole output of cerebellar cortex (GABA−). They integrate:
• Parallel fibers (granule cells) — sensorimotor context (thousands of synapses)
• Climbing fibers (inferior olive) — error signal / motor learning (1 powerful synapse)
Ataxia mechanism: Purkinje cell loss → DCN disinhibited → uncontrolled/oscillating output → Dysmetria, oscillatory tremor, ataxic gait

3 Cerebellar Syndromes

CMS — Cerebellar Motor Syndrome: Dysmetria, kinetic tremor, asynergia, adiadochokinesis, dyschronometria

⭐ High-Yield Exam Points — Cerebellar
  • Purkinje cells are GABA-ergic (inhibitory) — their loss → DCN disinhibited → ataxia
  • Climbing fibers from inferior olive = error signal for motor learning (1 per Purkinje)
  • Dorsal spinocerebellar = ipsilateral unconscious proprioception
  • Cerebellar ataxia: Normal JPS, negative Romberg (distinguish from sensory ataxia)
  • Nystagmus in cerebellar: Gaze-evoked nystagmus — fast phase toward gaze direction
🧍 Parkinson's Disease — In Depth
Pathophysiology · MDS Criteria · Natural Course · Motor Complications
📊 Levodopa Therapeutic Window — Motor Complications Simulator
Early PD: Wide therapeutic window — doses stay within therapeutic zone. Patient has smooth ON time throughout the day.
⭐ Exam Points — Levodopa & Motor Complications
  • 90% have motor fluctuations at 10 years | 60% have dyskinesia at 10 years
  • Peak-dose dyskinesia → reduce each LD dose (may need to increase frequency)
  • Wearing-off → increase LD frequency, or add DA/MAO-Bi/COMT-i
  • Delayed-on → pre-meal LD, add prokinetics, crushed tablet
  • Abrupt LD withdrawal → Parkinsonism Hyperpyrexia Syndrome (like NMS)
  • LD absorbed via LNAA transporter at jejunum → high protein meals delay absorption
🔬 MDS 2015 Diagnostic Criteria — PD

Diagnosis Flow

Step 1: Confirm parkinsonism → Bradykinesia is mandatory + rest tremor OR rigidity
Absolute Exclusions (any ONE = not PD):
Cerebellar abnormalities · Vertical gaze palsy (downgaze) · FTD dementia <3yr · Lower-limb parkinsonism only >3yr · Antidopaminergic drug exposure · No levodopa response · Cortical sensory loss/apraxia

Supportive Criteria (need ≥2)

  • Dramatic, durable levodopa response
  • Levodopa-induced dyskinesia present
  • Resting tremor on clinical examination
  • Olfactory loss (hyposmia)
  • MIBG cardiac sympathetic denervation
Sensitivity 94.5% / Specificity 88.5% — memorize these numbers!

⭐ Exam Points — PD Diagnosis
  • MDS Criteria: Sens 94.5%, Spec 88.5%
  • Bradykinesia = mandatory core feature
  • Pupil-sparing CN III palsy in DM → spares pupillomotor fibers (peripheral)
  • PD vs DLB: Dementia onset >1yr after motor = PDD; <1yr or concurrent = DLB
  • Olfactory loss is an early prodromal + supportive feature of PD

🔴 Atypical Parkinsonism
PSP · MSA · CBD · DLB — Pathology, Clinical Features & Differentiation
🔬 Molecular Pathology

🧬 Tauopathies (4R-tau)

PSP — tufted astrocytes, globose tangles in basal ganglia + brainstem. Tau accumulates around blood vessels.

CBD — astrocytic plaques, ballooned neurons, asymmetric cortex + basal ganglia

4R-tau isoforms aggregate selectively in neurons + glia. Misfolded tau cannot stabilize microtubules → neurodegeneration

🧬 Synucleinopathies (α-syn)

MSA — Glial Cytoplasmic Inclusions (GCIs) in oligodendrocytes → white matter degeneration. Striatonigral + olivopontocerebellar systems.

DLB — Lewy bodies in cortical neurons (same protein as PD but cortex-first)

α-synuclein spreads prion-like through neurons. In MSA, oligodendrocyte involvement is unique (not seen in PD/DLB)
📋 Clinical Differentiation Cards
PSP
Progressive Supranuclear Palsy · 4R-Tau
Core:
• Vertical gaze palsy (↓gaze first)
• Early backward falls (<3yr)
• Symmetric rigid parkinsonism
• Axial rigidity > limb

Face:
• Procerus sign (surprised/worried stare)
• Reduced blink rate, "reptilian stare"
• Hypometric vertical saccades

Cognitive:
• Frontal-subcortical syndrome
• Pseudobulbar affect

MRI: "Hummingbird sign" (midbrain atrophy on sagittal T1); "Morning Glory sign" (coronal)

LD response: Poor/none
Survival: ~7 yr
MSA
Multiple System Atrophy · α-Synuclein (GCIs)
Two subtypes:
• MSA-P: Striatonigral (parkinsonism dominant)
• MSA-C: Olivopontocerebellar (cerebellar dominant)

Core:
• Prominent early autonomic failure:
— Orthostatic hypotension
— Urinary incontinence/retention
— Erectile dysfunction
• Parkinsonism + cerebellar signs
• Inspiratory stridor (sleep)
• Anterocollis (forward neck lean)

MRI:
• "Hot cross bun" sign (T2, pons)
• Putaminal hypointensity + lateral rim
• Middle cerebellar peduncle T2↑

LD response: Partial, then wanes
Survival: ~6–9 yr
CBD / CBS
Corticobasal Degeneration · 4R-Tau
Note: CBD = pathology; CBS = clinical syndrome (CBS can arise from PSP, AD, FTLD)

CBS features:
• Asymmetric limb rigidity/apraxia
• Alien limb phenomenon
• Limb dystonia / myoclonus
• Cortical sensory loss (astereognosis)
• Apraxia of speech
• Eye movement apraxia

MRI: Asymmetric parietal cortical atrophy; tau PET asymmetric

DaTscan: Asymmetric DAT deficit

LD response: Poor
Survival: ~6–8 yr
DLB
Dementia with Lewy Bodies · α-Synuclein
Core features (≥2 = probable DLB):
• Fluctuating cognition
• Recurrent visual hallucinations (detailed)
• REM sleep behavior disorder
• Parkinsonism (concurrent or after dementia)

Supportive:
• ⚠️ Severe neuroleptic sensitivity
• Repeated falls/syncope
• Depression/anxiety
• Autonomic dysfunction

Biomarkers:
• DaTscan: ↓ striatal uptake
• MIBG: ↓ cardiac uptake
• EEG: periodic sharp waves

Key rule: Dementia within 1yr of parkinsonism = DLB (vs PDD = dementia >1yr after motor onset)
⭐ Exam Points — Atypical Parkinsonism
  • PSP: Vertical gaze palsy (downgaze first) + backward falls + "hummingbird sign" MRI
  • MSA: "Hot cross bun" sign on T2 MRI pons + severe early autonomic failure
  • CBD: Alien limb + asymmetric apraxia + cortical sensory loss
  • DLB: ⚠️ Avoid typical antipsychotics (severe neuroleptic sensitivity → can be fatal)
  • Tauopathies = PSP + CBD; Synucleinopathies = PD + MSA + DLB
  • MSA GCIs are in oligodendrocytes (unique — not found in other synucleinopathies)
  • All atypical PD: more rapid progression than idiopathic PD

🔍 Four Types of Ataxia — Comparison
🔵 Cerebellar
Mechanism: Purkinje cell loss → DCN disinhibited → oscillating uncontrolled output

✓ Dysmetria (FNF, heel-shin)
✓ Dysdiadochokinesia
✓ Scanning speech
✓ Gaze-evoked nystagmus
✓ Wide-based gait + impaired tandem
✗ Normal JPS
✗ Romberg negative

Causes: SCA, MSA-C, paraneoplastic, alcohol, Wernicke's, Wilson's
🟠 Sensory
Mechanism: Large-fiber neuropathy or dorsal column disease → loss of proprioceptive feedback

✓ Steppage / stomping gait
✓ Romberg POSITIVE
✓ Impaired JPS + vibration
✓ Worsens in dark
✗ No nystagmus
✗ No scanning speech
✗ Normal cerebellar tests

Causes: Vit B12/E deficiency, CIDP, tabes dorsalis, Friedreich's, SCD
🟢 Vestibular
Mechanism: Loss of vestibular spatial orientation input → cannot maintain posture

✓ Gait instability
✓ Vertigo / dizziness
✓ Nystagmus (variable)
✓ Veers/falls to one side
✗ No limb dysmetria
✗ No speech involvement
✗ Normal JPS

Causes: Vestibular neuritis, BPPV, Menière's, acoustic neuroma, brainstem stroke
🟣 Frontal
Mechanism: Frontal-subcortical circuit disruption → impaired gait initiation/apraxia

✓ "Glue feet" / freezing of gait
✓ Short shuffling steps
✓ Postural instability
✓ Executive dysfunction
✗ No scanning speech
✗ No nystagmus
✗ Normal limb coordination

Causes: NPH (Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus), lacunar disease, frontal tumor
⭐ Exam Points — Ataxia Types
  • Only sensory ataxia has positive Romberg | Only cerebellar ataxia has nystagmus + scanning speech + dysmetria
  • Wernicke's complete triad (ataxia + ophthalmoplegia + confusion) = only ~20% — give thiamine empirically!
  • Metronidazole → T2 hyperintensity at dentate nuclei (reversible)
  • Frontal ataxia (NPH triad): Wet, Wacky, Wobbly — urinary incontinence + dementia + gait apraxia
  • Cerebellar ataxia without family history, onset >40yr, no clear cause → ~15% MSA-C
🧬 Spinocerebellar Ataxia (SCA) Genetics

PolyQ Mechanism

CAG repeat expansion in coding region → mutant protein with expanded polyglutamine → toxic gain-of-function → protein aggregation + UPS disruption + transcriptional dysregulation + Ca²⁺ signaling → Purkinje cell death
Genetic Anticipation: CAG repeats expand in successive generations (especially paternal) → earlier onset + more severe disease in offspring

Friedreich's Ataxia

Gene: FXN (frataxin) — GAA trinucleotide repeat expansion (intronic, autosomal recessive)
Frataxin ↓ → mitochondrial iron accumulation → oxidative stress → Purkinje + DRG neuron + cardiomyocyte death

Features: Onset <25yr · Sensorimotor ataxia · Areflexia · Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy · Diabetes · Scoliosis · Pes cavus

MRI: Spinal cord atrophy (cervical)
Treatment: Omaveloxolone (Nrf2 activator, 2023) · Cardiac monitoring essential

  • SCA3 = most common SCA worldwide (Machado-Joseph disease)
  • Friedreich's: GAA repeat (not CAG); autosomal recessive; cardiomyopathy → #1 cause of death

Essential Tremor: Bilateral postural/kinetic, arm > head > voice. Alcohol-responsive. Rx: propranolol, primidone, zonisamide.

💃 Chorea

Definition: Random, flowing, dance-like; non-rhythmic; unpredictable

Pathophysiology: Indirect pathway ↓↓ (opposite of PD) → GPi underactive → Thalamus disinhibited → excess cortical excitation

Key Causes:

  • Huntington's disease — CAG in HTT; choreiform + dementia + psychiatric
  • Sydenham's chorea — post-streptococcal; OCP-related
  • SLE, antiphospholipid syndrome
  • Drug-induced (levodopa, antipsychotics)
  • Thyrotoxicosis, polycythemia vera
⭐ Exam — Chorea
  • Huntington's: CAG in HTT gene, chr 4; autosomal dominant; anticipation
  • Chorea = indirect pathway ↓ = opposite of PD
  • Levodopa excess → peak-dose choreiform dyskinesia

⚡ Myoclonus & Dystonia

Myoclonus: Sudden, brief, shock-like jerk

  • Cortical — focal seizure origin; EEG correlate
  • Subcortical/brainstem — palatal myoclonus
  • Spinal — propriospinal myoclonus
  • Action (Lance-Adams) — post-hypoxic
  • Metabolic: uremia, hepatic encephalopathy

Dystonia: Sustained/intermittent muscle contractions → abnormal posture

  • Geste antagoniste (sensory trick) = pathognomonic clue
  • Cervical dystonia = most common focal dystonia
  • Treatment: Botulinum toxin (1st line focal)
  • Generalized: GPi-DBS; trihexyphenidyl
⭐ Exam — Dystonia
  • Geste antagoniste = sensory trick that temporarily relieves dystonia → diagnostic clue
  • Botulinum toxin: cleaves SNAP-25 (BoNT-A) → blocks ACh release → focal muscle relaxation, lasts 3–4 months
🟤 Wilson's Disease — Copper Metabolism
ATP7B mutation · Copper overload · Liver + Brain + Eye + Kidney
⚗️ Copper Pathway — Normal vs Wilson's
Hepatic Copper Metabolism Simulator

Pathophysiology

ATP7B encodes Cu-transporting ATPase in hepatocytes. Normally: incorporates Cu into ceruloplasmin + exports excess Cu into bile. In Wilson's: ATP7B ↓ → Cu cannot be exported → accumulates in liver → brain → cornea → kidney

⭐ Exam Points — Wilson's Disease
  • Always suspect in young patient with movement disorder + liver disease + psychiatric symptoms
  • KF rings present in 95% with neurological WD; can be absent in hepatic-only
  • Ceruloplasmin ↓ + urine Cu ↑ → classic; but ceruloplasmin can be normal in fulminant (acute phase reactant)
  • Paradoxical neurological worsening after starting chelation → Cu redistribution; prevent by slow titration, prefer trientine
  • Trientine = preferred 1st line (safer than penicillamine); do NOT combine with iron
  • Zinc = 1st line for asymptomatic / maintenance
💊 Wilson's Treatment Algorithm

Symptomatic

1st line: Trientine (900mg–2g/day in 2–3 divided doses)
Safer, less paradoxical worsening. Do not combine with iron.
Alternative: D-Penicillamine (0.5–2g/day)
+ Pyridoxine (B6). ⚠️ 10% paradoxical neurological worsening. Safe in pregnancy.

Asymptomatic / Maintenance

Zinc salts 150mg elemental Zn/day (50mg TID)
↑ Intestinal metallothionein → traps Cu in enterocytes → excreted with shedding. Take on empty stomach. SE: GI irritation.
Treatment goal: 24h urine Cu 200–500 μg/day (initial chelation); normalize within 2 days of stopping chelator (maintenance monitoring)

Special Situations

Fulminant liver failure: Liver transplantation — corrects metabolic defect; neurological improvement possible
Paradoxical worsening: Cu redistribution from chelation → oxidative damage. Risk: severe neurological involvement, BS/thalamic MRI lesions. Rx: titrate chelator down slowly.

⭐ Master Exam Cheat Sheet — Movement Disorders

Key Numbers to Memorize

  • SNc neuron loss at motor onset: 50–60%
  • MDS PD criteria: sens 94.5% / spec 88.5%
  • Motor fluctuations at 10yr: 90%
  • Dyskinesia at 10yr: 60%
  • Wernicke's complete triad: ~20%
  • KF rings in neurological WD: 95%
  • MSA-C among late-onset ataxia: ~15%
  • Riluzole for SCA: SARA ↓ ~2.7 pts at 12 months

Key Pathology Associations

  • PD + DLB + MSA → α-synuclein
  • PSP + CBD → 4R-tau
  • MSA GCIs → oligodendrocytes (unique)
  • DLB → avoid antipsychotics (neuroleptic sensitivity)
  • Anti-Yo → Breast/Ovary (poor immunotherapy response)
  • Anti-Tr → Hodgkin lymphoma
  • Huntington's → CAG in HTT (chr 4)
  • Wilson's → ATP7B (chr 13q)

High-Risk Drug Interactions

  • MAO-B inhibitors + SSRIs/tramadol/meperidine → Serotonin syndrome
  • Selegiline high dose → MAO-A too → tyramine hypertension
  • Ropinirole + ciprofloxacin → ↑ ropinirole (CYP1A2)
  • Trientine + iron → mutual chelation — don't combine
  • Levodopa + high-protein meal → delayed absorption
  • Antipsychotics in DLB → severe, potentially fatal reaction
  • D-Penicillamine → watch for drug-induced lupus, MG, nephrotic
  • Abrupt LD withdrawal → Parkinsonism Hyperpyrexia (treat like NMS)
